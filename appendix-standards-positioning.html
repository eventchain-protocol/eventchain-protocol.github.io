Appendix: Comparison Tables

Modified

October 14, 2026

This appendix holds the comparison tables that 3  Consensus by Comparison relies on and shows how EventChain relates to existing transparency and provenance systems.

Standards Lineage

EventChain sits in a lineage of cryptographic accountability systems. Haber and Stornetta established hash-linked time-stamping as a way to make document history tamper-evident without a conventional record-keeper (Haber and Stornetta 1991). Certificate Transparency and Trillian turned append-only transparency logs into operational infrastructure for public certificates (Laurie et al. 2013; Google 2026). SCITT generalises transparency receipts for supply-chain statements using COSE/CBOR and external transparency services (Birkholz et al. 2026). KERI focuses on self-certifying identifiers and key-event logs for decentralised identity (Smith 2019). Parakeet demonstrates privacy-preserving transparency through public commitments, private querying, and verifiable proof (Malvai et al. 2023). TrustChain shows that a blockchain can preserve data while leaving truth at origin unresolved (Malik et al. 2019).

EventChain keeps the chained hash and the published heartbeat from these systems. What it adds is a key for every signer and a file the parties keep themselves.

SCITT and EventChain operate at different layers. SCITT answers whether a transparency service registered a signed statement; its receipt can accompany the statement for verification (Birkholz et al. 2026, sec. 7). EventChain preserves producer-signed entries and their sequence in a portable file.
